# Market and competition memo: private-pay online physiotherapy in DACH

**Research date:** 12 September 2026. **Scope:** Germany, Austria, Switzerland; self-pay and private-insurance customers; live video care with digital exercise support. This is commercial research, not legal advice. Prices and availability are dated observations from provider pages and may change.

## Decision signal

**Observed:** Musculoskeletal need is large in all three markets. Germany recorded 1,517,912 hospital patients with an M00-M99 musculoskeletal diagnosis in 2024, while the Swiss Federal Statistical Office reports back or neck pain as the most common physical complaint, affecting 45% of the population in its cited health survey. Austria's official 2021 health yearbook recorded 22,997 hospital cases coded as back pain. Sources: [Destatis 2024 hospital diagnoses](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Gesellschaft-Umwelt/Gesundheit/Krankenhauser/Tabellen/diagnose-kapitel-geschlecht-krankenhaeuser.html), [BFS population health](https://www.swissstats.bfs.admin.ch/data/webviewer/appId/ch.admin.bfs.swissstat/article/issue241415402400-03/package), [Statistics Austria health yearbook 2021](https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Gesundheitsstatistik-JB_2021_Web-barrierefrei.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12).

**Observed:** The direct online market is fragmented. It consists mainly of individual practices selling video appointments, hybrid rehab coaching, and digital exercise subscriptions. A large adjacent competitor, Kaia, offers a reimbursed app for back pain, but explicitly says its program is not physiotherapy and requires a diagnosis or insurer access. Sources: [DR PHYSIO online rehab](https://www.physio-dr.de/online-reha), [Daniel Ringhofer rehab coaching](https://www.daniel-ringhofer.at/), [Kaia](https://kaiahealth.de/rueckenschmerzen/) (accessed 2026-09-12).

**Inference:** There is room for a trusted, cross-border brand if it sells a defined outcome and care pathway rather than generic “online physiotherapy.” The strongest initial gap is likely structured, clinician-led support for persistent or recurrent musculoskeletal problems in digitally confident working adults, with explicit triage and a fast handoff to in-person care when video is insufficient. This is a hypothesis to test, not a measured market share claim.

**Why sports should not be the default:** Sports is easy to market and supports premium assessment, but visible competition is already strong and many buyers can substitute a coach, local physio, or content creator. Persistent work-related pain has broader demand and repeat need, while a clinician-led accountability service can sit between a low-cost app and a full local practice.

## Distribution implications from the competitive evidence

Competitors reveal three working acquisition patterns:

1. **High-intent search:** “online physiotherapy,” condition plus “online,” and “private physiotherapy” lead to provider pages. Build condition-specific pages with clinician credentials, suitability limits, price, insurance wording, and a short self-screen.
2. **Trust-led practitioner content:** Personal pages and free 15-minute calls reduce the barrier to a remote clinical service. Publish practical movement education and clinician introductions, then route to a paid assessment.
3. **Partnership distribution:** Kaia shows the power of insurer access; local providers show referral and practice trust. For a self-pay service, target employers with high desk-work exposure, orthopedic/surgical practices for post-discharge follow-up, gyms and running clubs, and private insurers only after reimbursement language is validated.

Recommended first funnel: symptom or goal page → 2-minute suitability screen → free 15-minute fit call or paid assessment → 4- or 6-week package → outcome reassessment and referral/continuation. Track qualified visits, screen completion, booked assessment, assessment-to-package conversion, completion, outcome change, refund rate, and referral rate.

## Key risks and tests

| Risk | What must be true | Fast test and kill signal |
|---|---|---|
| Customers will pay without guaranteed reimbursement | At least one segment values convenience, accountability, and speed enough to buy | Run a landing-page smoke test with real payment intent. Kill or reposition if fewer than 5% of qualified leads book a paid assessment after 200 qualified visits, or if 10 interviews reveal no recent self-pay behavior. |
| Video is clinically adequate for the chosen condition | Remote assessment and exercise progression produce useful outcomes without unsafe delay | Concierge pilot with 10-15 screened clients and clinician-rated suitability. Stop the indication if red-flag or escalation misses occur, or completion is below 60%. |
| CAC can support clinician time | Package contribution margin remains positive after acquisition and support | Test €1,000-€2,000 across two intent-led channels. Pause a channel if booked assessment CAC exceeds 40% of first-package revenue after 30 days. These are proposed thresholds, not observed benchmarks. |
| Cross-border delivery is operationally clean | Practitioner authorization, location, records, privacy, tax, and insurance statements work in each country | Obtain written counsel answers before accepting customers or clinicians across borders. Do not market “DACH-wide” until the operating country and clinical responsibility are fixed. |
| The offer is differentiated from apps and solo practices | Buyers can explain why they choose this service | Test three messages: persistent work pain, post-discharge rehab, and sports return. Advance the message with the highest paid-assessment conversion and completion, not click-through alone. |

## Recommendation to the main venture analysis

**Proceed with constraints and validate one country first.** Start in Germany or Austria with one German-language wedge around persistent/recurrent back and neck pain for working adults, staffed by locally authorized clinicians. Sell a short package with an initial video assessment, scheduled check-ins, an exercise plan, asynchronous accountability, and explicit referral rules. Keep sports as a channel and proof point. Add Austria and Switzerland only after clinical safety, paid conversion, package completion, and unit economics pass the pre-set thresholds.

The evidence supports demand and price anchors, but it does not prove private-pay willingness at scale, cross-border legality, or clinical outcomes. The next research pass should verify professional authorization and telehealth rules per country, interview at least 10 recent self-pay buyers, and compare live paid offers through a controlled landing-page test.

## Source quality and limits

- **Primary/official:** Destatis, RKI, Statistics Austria, BFS, WHO, ÖGK, Physio Austria, and Swiss BAG pages were used for burden, reimbursement context, or system facts.
- **Company-owned observations:** Competitor features and prices come from provider pages and booking pages, retrieved 2026-09-12. They are snapshots, not audited revenue or market-share data.
- **Known gap:** No reliable public, comparable DACH estimate of the private-pay online physiotherapy market, CAC, conversion, or retention was found in this pass. Do not use a top-down market-size number without a defined methodology.
