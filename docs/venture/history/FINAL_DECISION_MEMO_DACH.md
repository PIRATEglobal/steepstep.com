# Founder decision memo: online physiotherapy in DACH

**Date:** 12 September 2026
**Decision status:** Analysis complete. Proceed with constraints, subject to the German/Austrian authorization gate
**Scope:** Consumer self-pay and privately insured customers; live video care, digital exercise guidance, and fixed packages. This is a commercial decision memo, not legal advice.

## The decision

Run a 90-day concierge pilot in one country, with one primary clinical wedge and no custom platform. Start with a sports/MSK cohort. Run the desk-worker neck/back comparator only as a separately tagged second cohort if the first cohort is clinically appropriate and operationally stable. Default to Germany only if written counsel confirms that the proposed clinician and entity arrangement permits the intended direct-access activity for German residents. If that answer is unclear or negative, start in Austria with Austrian-qualified clinicians and the same narrow service. Keep Switzerland as the third market after a named-canton review.

The commercial thesis is credible. Access is constrained, patients already understand digital care, and observed private prices support a €449–€549 package. The venture is not ready for a broad DACH launch because legal responsibility follows the patient and clinician context, remote care has clinical limits, and the base package economics are thin.

The first product should be clinician-led remote musculoskeletal care with a measurable goal, not a general exercise library. Sports is the team’s strongest credibility wedge. Persistent neck/back pain among working adults is the broader demand test. Run both messages in the same pilot and choose by paid, safe, profitable behavior.

## What the evidence says

Germany has the largest demand pool and visible competition. Destatis recorded 1,517,912 hospital patients with an M00–M99 musculoskeletal diagnosis in 2024. The Federal Employment Agency reports a 168-day median vacancy duration for physiotherapy roles in 2025, a supply constraint signal. These figures show need and constrained supply, not private-pay demand. [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Gesellschaft-Umwelt/Gesundheit/Krankenhaeuser/Tabellen/diagnose-kapitel-geschlecht-krankenhaeuser.html), [BA Arbeitsmarktmonitor](https://arbeitsmarktmonitor.arbeitsagentur.de/faktencheck/fachkraefte/tabelle/515/92236/3/) (accessed 12 September 2026).

Austria is smaller but has a clearer statutory telemedicine reference for MTD professionals. Austria’s 2021 health yearbook records 22,997 hospital cases coded as back pain and 195,179 M00–M99 cases. Switzerland has visible private price anchors, but canton permits and the patient’s location create a higher entry burden. BFS reports back or neck pain affecting 45% of the population in its cited survey. The datasets are not comparable prevalence measures, and the Swiss price observations do not prove willingness to pay at scale. [Statistics Austria](https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Gesundheitsstatistik-JB_2021_Web-barrierefrei.pdf), [BFS](https://www.swissstats.bfs.admin.ch/data/webviewer/appId/ch.admin.bfs.swissstat/article/issue241415402400-03/package) (accessed 12 September 2026).

Competition validates the category but leaves room for a focused offer. “Distance” below means similarity of the observed offer and price, not market share, reach, conversion, retention, or clinical outcomes:

## Country sequence and legal gate

| Country | Why it could work | Gate before selling care | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | Largest pool, search demand, established digital-care familiarity, sports communities | Confirm direct-access authority under MPhG and Heilpraktikergesetz interaction, clinician location, entity model, professional insurance, remote-treatment advertising, consumer package terms, and health-data controls | Default first only with written authorization answer. |
| Austria | MTDG §28(3) expressly permits telemedicine when professionally defensible and lege artis, with communication-medium information; smaller direct online field | Confirm MTD registration/recognition, patient-location and establishment rules, contractor model, insurance, records, advertising, tax, and consumer terms | Safer fallback first country if Germany remains uncertain. |
| Switzerland | High price anchors and clear online-care examples | Confirm target canton Berufsausübungsbewilligung, recognized qualification, entity and immigration/tax treatment, Swiss data duties, and reimbursement wording | Third market, one canton at a time. |

Germany’s protected physiotherapy title does not by itself settle independent diagnosis and treatment for a self-pay direct-access service. The Heilpraktikergesetz defines healing practice broadly. Germany also has specific advertising restrictions for remote diagnosis or treatment. Austria’s telemedicine permission is conditional and does not remove scope, triage, or cross-border duties. Swiss authorization is materially canton-sensitive. Treat these as counsel questions until written answers exist.

## Segment choice: sports versus desk-worker pain

Use sequential, separately tagged cohorts with one clinical workflow and two landing pages. Do not combine the cohorts in the RAT. Run 20 eligible assessed prospects per segment before comparing conversion. Switch to desk-worker pain only if its paid conversion is at least 25% higher than sports and it does not miss the safety, completion, outcome, or margin gates.

| Test | Sports/MSK, default | Desk-worker neck/back pain, comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Active adults 25–55 who want to return to running, cycling, football, racket sport, or gym | Working adults 25–55 with persistent/recurrent pain and travel or scheduling friction |
| Promise | “A clinician-adjusted plan to get you back to your sport with measurable progression.” | “A structured plan and weekly expert adjustment to get daily movement and work back under control.” |
| Proof | Readiness tests, load progression, movement feedback, coach handoff | Function-at-work measure, adherence, pacing, ergonomic behavior |
| Why test | Strong team expertise and higher urgency | Larger need signal and potentially broader SEO volume |
| Exclude | Acute red flags, cases needing hands-on examination, unstable post-op care | Same clinical exclusions |

Default to sports because the team can earn trust faster there. Test desk-worker pain only after the sports cohort is appropriate and stable, using a fresh 20 eligible assessed prospects. Test women’s health, pelvic floor, paediatric, neuro, and complex post-operative care only as research tracks until specialist governance and referral pathways exist.

## Service and operating model

Sell a six-week “Performance Reset” at a €449 founding price, moving toward €549 only after willingness to pay is observed. Include a pre-visit screen, 60-minute video assessment, two to four high-value exercises with progression rules, four 30-minute reviews, capped weekly asynchronous form feedback, and a written next-step summary. Use secure existing video, scheduling, payment, and documentation tools. Build software only where measured repetition saves clinician time or improves adherence.

The intake must stop purchase and route to local care for emergency symptoms, suspected fracture or serious pathology, cases requiring hands-on examination, unstable post-operative cases, or any case the clinician cannot assess safely online. Use one clinical lead and one backup clinician, a standard triage form, documentation minimum, incident log, and peer review for the first 20 cases.

## Economics and the required change

The reproducible model is in [venture_unit_economics.py](../model/venture_unit_economics.py) and [venture_scenarios.csv](../model/venture_scenarios.csv).

| Scenario | Price | Clinician hours | CAC | First-order contribution | Two-cycle LTV:CAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Downside | €349 | 4.5 | €140 | -€212 | -1.4x |
| Base | €449 | 4.0 | €70 | €55 | 1.2x |
| Upside | €549 | 3.25 | €55 | €247 | 5.5x |

Assumptions: clinician cost €70/hour, support 0.5 hour/package at €25/hour, payment fees 2%, refunds 5%, continuation price €249, repeat purchase 30%, and €3,000 monthly fixed overhead. The base case does not justify paid scale. A €3,000 fixed stack needs about 36 base-case packages per month, above the modeled 27-package clinician capacity.

The €150 contribution gate requires a deliberate change from base assumptions. At the base €449 price, 5% refunds, €70/hour clinician cost, and €70 CAC, clinician time must fall from four hours to about 2.64 hours to produce €150 first-order contribution. At four hours, CAC would need to be about -€25, which is impossible. Therefore use one of these tested paths: €549 price with ≤3.25 clinician hours and ≤€55 CAC, €449 price with materially lower delivery time plus partner/organic CAC, or a higher-value continuation block. Do not claim that the current base model passes the gate.

## RAT and validation gates

The original RAT combined several outcomes. Use it as a north-star, but make the launch decision through separate gates. “20” means 20 prospects who pass the clinical inclusion screen and complete the assessment. Track the full lead funnel separately. If fewer than 20 eligible assessed prospects are reached, the conversion gate is unproven, not passed.

Pass the pilot only if all are true:

- **Safety and referral:** zero preventable serious incidents, all referrals and near misses logged, and clinical-lead sign-off. A serious incident stops the cohort and is not averaged against revenue.
- **Paid conversion:** at least 8/20 eligible assessed prospects purchase at €399 or more.
- **Delivery:** median clinician time ≤4.0 hours/package; >4.5 hours triggers redesign.
- **Adherence:** at least 70% complete planned touchpoints, with the denominator fixed before delivery.
- **Outcome:** at least 60% show improvement on a patient-specific functional measure, with the measure and baseline fixed before delivery.
- **Economics:** realised first-order contribution ≥€150, or a documented price/time/CAC change shows a credible path there without quality loss.
- **Customer quality:** refunds ≤8%, and continuation or qualified referral intent ≥30%.

Fewer than 4 purchases from 20 eligible assessed prospects triggers a segment or promise change. If 4–7 purchase, continue only as a learning pilot. If remote suitability is below 70% or a preventable serious incident occurs, narrow the indication and obtain clinical review before more sales. Paid search stays capped and pauses above €120 CAC without improvement. The usual 3x LTV:CAC ratio is a management heuristic, not evidence of this market.

## 30/60/90-day plan

**Days 0–30:** obtain written country authorization answers, recruit clinical lead, define triage and outcomes, conduct 15 past-behavior interviews, build two landing pages, and set up secure manual booking/payment. Create a claim register recording exact wording, evidence, reviewer, and channel for “return to sport,” “schmerzfrei,” “evidenzbasiert,” testimonials, self-check questions, and creator/partner claims. No app or three-country checkout.

**Days 31–60:** deliver 10–20 packages, log every minute, outcome, referral, refund, and complaint; recruit 10 sports and clinical referral partners; run the sports and desk-worker A/B test. Keep paid tests below €1,000 per message until safety and checkout work.

**Days 61–90:** complete 20 eligible assessed prospects, add backup clinician and peer review, run up to €2,000 of controlled high-intent search for the winning segment, and prepare the next-country legal checklist. Expand only if the gates pass for three consecutive weeks of qualified demand.

An illustrative proposed validation cap is €15,000: €3,000 legal/privacy/insurance, €2,000 landing page and workflow, €5,000 delivery and research incentives, and up to €5,000 paid tests after the first ten safe paid cases. This is a planning estimate, not authorization to spend. Before committing any amount, add a cash schedule showing payment timing, clinician payouts, refunds, VAT/tax treatment, and fixed-cost timing. The model’s 36-package break-even is a cohort approximation because it uses expected repeat contribution; it is not same-month cash break-even.

## Questions for counsel before launch

Ask German, Austrian, and Swiss counsel to answer in writing: (1) may the proposed clinician diagnose and treat the target cases directly, given their qualification and location; (2) where is care legally supplied when clinician and patient are in different countries; (3) is the company itself a healthcare provider or platform, and who owns clinical records; (4) what registration, notification, establishment, contractor, professional-liability, and tax steps apply; (5) what remote-care and advertising claims are permitted; (6) how should package cancellation, withdrawal, expiry, refunds, and mixed live/digital content be structured; (7) what GDPR Art. 9/revDSG basis, DPIA, processor, retention, access, and breach controls are required; (8) does triage, diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or monitoring make the software a medical device under EU MDR or Swiss rules; (9) what private-insurance wording is permissible; and (10) for Switzerland, what changes by the first target canton?

## Recommendation

Proceed with a constrained pilot. Use Germany first only after direct-access authorization is confirmed. If Germany cannot be cleared quickly and cleanly, Austria is the better first operating jurisdiction because the MTDG telemedicine provision gives a clearer starting point, while still requiring local professional and establishment review. Use sports/MSK as the default segment, test desk-worker pain in parallel, and let paid conversion plus safe outcomes decide the wedge. Enter Switzerland third, canton by canton.

The venture earns the right to scale only after 20 eligible assessed prospects produce the RAT and the economic gate. If demand is real but contribution misses €150, change the package price, delivery time, or acquisition mix before buying scale. If willingness to pay or remote suitability fails after one focused sports test and one desk-worker test, stop or pivot before building a platform.
