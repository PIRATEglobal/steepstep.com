## Intended outcome

Produce an evidence-backed, decision-ready evaluation of whether and how a lean, fully online, private-pay or privately insured physiotherapy service can operate in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland, followed by a concrete launch plan and a clear recommendation. The evaluation must cover legal context, market and competition, operating model, distribution and online marketing, economics, risks, validation steps, and go/no-go criteria.

## Explicit requirements

- Geographic scope: Germany, Austria, Switzerland.
- Business model: online physiotherapy, remote appointments, digital-first and lean delivery.
- Commercial focus: self-paying customers and people whose private insurance may reimburse; public-insurance customers are outside the primary target.
- Product shape: simple purchase of packages and online sessions.
- Evaluate market viability, legal surroundings/context, competition, distance to competitors, risks, distribution, digital marketing, and how to approach risks.
- Deliver a thorough plan and a clear recommendation.
- Use relevant Resonance venture and strategy skills extensively.
- Create this brief first, then execute it.
- Delegate work to Luna Medium U Orchestrate and Review.

## Inferred details, with evidence

- The initial offer should be tested as a focused wedge rather than a broad clinical marketplace. Evidence: the user prioritizes lean operations, packages, and digital distribution. This remains reversible.
- Legal work should distinguish the three jurisdictions and the practitioner, service, advertising, privacy, consumer, tax, and cross-border dimensions. Evidence: “Germany, Austria and Switzerland” and “everything is done online.”
- The final recommendation should include staged validation before substantial build or spend. Evidence: the user asks whether it will work and asks for risks and how to approach them. This is a planning recommendation, not an additional promise of execution.
- “How far are there?” is interpreted as competitor proximity or maturity, including market positioning, reach, pricing, and differentiation. If the user meant geographic distance, flag this interpretation in the research.
- “People that have a certificate” is treated as licensed or otherwise legally qualified physiotherapists. This wording must be verified against each jurisdiction's protected-profession rules and not treated as a legal fact.

## Unresolved material decisions

These are research questions to resolve in the brief execution. They do not block the authorized market evaluation, but they must be called out before a launch decision:

- Whether the provider is a licensed physiotherapist in each relevant jurisdiction, employs local practitioners, or operates through a partner model.
- Whether customers and practitioners may be located across borders, and where the business entity and clinical responsibility will sit.
- Which conditions and customer segments are in scope, and which require in-person examination or referral.
- Whether asynchronous guidance, exercise plans, or digital therapeutic features are included alongside live video care.
- Target price, package length, capacity assumptions, and initial launch country/order.
- Whether the user wants an actual legal opinion or a commercial decision brief with counsel-ready questions. Recommendation: produce the latter and explicitly identify where local counsel is required.

## Deliverables

1. A dated, source-linked DACH market and customer analysis.
2. A jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal matrix covering professional authorization, telehealth/service rules, cross-border provision, advertising and consumer law, privacy and health data, records, informed consent, billing/tax, insurance reimbursement, and medical-device or software boundaries where relevant. Separate verified rules, uncertainty, and counsel questions.
3. A competitor map with direct and adjacent players, country coverage, clinical model, customer segment, pricing/package evidence, acquisition channels, strengths, gaps, and practical differentiation distance.
4. A business model and unit-economics model for package-based private pay, with explicit assumptions and sensitivity cases for price, clinician utilization, acquisition cost, conversion, retention, refunds, and support load.
5. A distribution plan covering positioning, SEO/GEO, paid search/social, partnerships, referrals, content, conversion path, trust proof, and measurement. Include prioritized experiments, budgets as ranges, and kill criteria.
6. An operating and risk plan covering clinical safety, triage/referral, quality, staffing, data/security, cross-border delivery, reputation, and platform dependency.
7. A staged validation and launch roadmap with milestones, owners, evidence thresholds, go/no-go criteria, and a clear recommendation: proceed, proceed with constraints, or stop.
8. A concise executive decision memo suitable for the founder, with sources and explicit caveats.

## Source and verification standard

- Browse current sources because legal rules, reimbursement, competitor offerings, prices, and platform policies change.
- Use primary sources first for law and regulation: official federal/cantonal/state authorities, professional chambers, regulators, data-protection authorities, and official legislation. Use qualified local legal or professional guidance to identify interpretation gaps.
- Use company-owned pages for competitor product and pricing claims, and label dated observations. Use reputable market or academic sources for demand estimates; disclose methodology and avoid unsupported market-size precision.
- Every material claim gets a nearby source, retrieval date, jurisdiction, and confidence level. Mark inference separately from observed fact.
- Do not present the output as legal advice. Convert unresolved issues into counsel-ready questions and identify the advisor needed.
- Verify calculations from an explicit assumptions table or reproducible spreadsheet/script. Test downside, base, and upside cases.

## Success checks

- All three jurisdictions are covered separately, with cross-border implications addressed.
- The target customer and private-pay package model remain central throughout.
- Competition is specific and decision-useful, not a generic list.
- Legal constraints and unknowns are separated from business recommendations.
- Distribution has measurable experiments and kill criteria.
- Risks have mitigations, owners, and validation evidence.
- The recommendation follows from explicit assumptions and evidence, with a practical next 30/60/90-day plan.
- No claim relies on stale or uncited information; source dates are visible.
