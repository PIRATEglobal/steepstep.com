# Venture and go-to-market recommendation, online physiotherapy DACH

**Prepared:** 12 September 2026
**Scope:** private-pay and privately insured customers in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland.
**Decision:** **Proceed with constraints.** Start with a Germany-first concierge pilot for remote-suitable musculoskeletal and sports rehabilitation. Do not launch three countries or build a full platform before paid demand, clinical safety, and contribution margin are proven.

This is a commercial decision brief, not legal advice or a clinical protocol. The legal workstream must confirm the practitioner, entity, tax, insurance, data, advertising, and cross-border setup before selling care.

## 1. Venture thesis

The customer job is: “I want a credible plan and a real expert to keep me progressing between appointments, without travel, waiting, or generic app exercises.” The strongest initial segment is an active adult with a non-urgent sports or musculoskeletal problem who can perform a safe self-assessment at home, values a fast return to training, and can pay €350–€550 for a structured block.

The wedge is a clinician-led six-week return-to-sport or persistent-pain programme. It combines a video assessment, a personalised plan, short follow-ups, asynchronous form review, and a clear escalation path to in-person care. The customer buys an outcome-oriented package, not an open-ended marketplace subscription.

Why this can work:

- Access is a real pain. Germany’s Federal Employment Agency reports a 168-day median vacancy duration and a 0.8 job-seeker-to-position ratio for physiotherapy roles in 2025, a useful indicator of constrained supply, though it does not prove consumer willingness to pay. [BA Arbeitsmarktmonitor](https://arbeitsmarktmonitor.arbeitsagentur.de/faktencheck/fachkraefte/tabelle/515/92236/3/) (retrieved 2026-09-12, high confidence for the labour-market figures).
- Digital physiotherapy is already accepted as a delivery mode in defined German statutory arrangements. TK describes video assessment, personalised exercise plans, and an app through Herodikos plus. [Techniker Krankenkasse](https://www.tk.de/techniker/versicherung/tk-leistungen/weitere-leistungen/igv-vertraege/individuelle-telephysiotherapie-herodikos-plus-2081502) (retrieved 2026-09-12, high confidence for the described offer).
- Private-pay price headroom exists. A German online-rehab provider lists €150 for a 60-minute assessment, €150 for a single appointment, and €300 per month for ongoing support. [DR PHYSIO](https://www.physio-dr.de/online-reha) (retrieved 2026-09-12, high confidence for observed prices).
- The product can be more than a video call. The winning experience is fast triage, specific progression, video feedback, and accountability. A generic exercise library is easy to copy and already competes with insurer-funded apps.

The main reason for restraint is economics. At a €449 package, four clinician hours, €70 clinician cost per hour, 5% refunds, €70 CAC, and 30% repeat purchase, first-order contribution is only about €55 and two-cycle LTV:CAC is about 1.2x. This is not ready for paid scale. The offer must either reduce clinician time, raise price, improve organic/referral acquisition, or increase repeat value.

## 3. Market gaps and positioning distance

The market is not empty. That is helpful: customers already understand the category. The gap is a narrow operating promise that existing generalist offers do not own.

| Gap | Evidence or signal | Product response | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sports-specific return to performance | DR PHYSIO lists running and tendon diagnoses, but the observed offer remains a broad online-rehab service. | Build sport and goal-specific tracks, readiness tests, load progression, and coach-friendly summaries. | Medium, validate in interviews. |
| Between-visit accountability | Insurer programmes provide plans and apps, while independent practices sell time. | Async video review and weekly plan adjustment as the core paid value. | Medium. |
| Cross-border German-language continuity | DACH customers move, travel, and work across borders, but professional authorization and place-of-care rules may restrict delivery. | Treat this as a later distribution segment, only after counsel maps each route. | Low until legal review. |
| Expats and multilingual athletes | English-language search demand is plausible in major DACH cities, but demand and reimbursement are unproven. | Test German and English landing pages with the same clinical offer. | Low to medium. |
| Persistent pain with a clear self-management pathway | Larger pain platforms and apps tend to be generic; many patients need education, pacing, and adherence more than more content. | A clinician-led programme with outcome tracking and explicit red-flag triage. | Medium. |
| Post-discharge continuity | Hospitals and practices often have a gap after formal treatment; no claim of systematic demand is made here. | Partner pilot with one sports physician, clinic, or coach, with referral tracking. | Low until partner interviews. |
| Women’s health, postpartum, pelvic floor, neuro, and complex post-op | Potentially attractive gaps, but assessment and safety needs are higher and remote suitability varies. | Research only. Do not include in the first offer without specialist governance. | Low. |

The recommended position is “remote sports physiotherapy with measurable progression,” not “online physiotherapy for everyone.” Adjacent markets can be tested after the sports wedge reaches repeatable economics. The first adjacent test should be desk-worker neck/back pain because the demand is broad and remote-compatible. Women’s health, paediatric, neurological, and complex post-operative care should stay outside the launch scope until a specialist and referral system exist.

## 4. Offer and operating model

### Launch package

**Performance Reset, six weeks, €449 pilot price, €549 target price**

1. Pre-visit questionnaire, consent, red-flag screen, and camera/setup check.
2. 60-minute video assessment with movement tests that are safe to perform remotely.
3. Personal plan with two to four high-value exercises and progression rules, delivered in a secure patient area.
4. Four 30-minute video reviews.
5. Weekly asynchronous form check, capped at a defined time budget.
6. Written summary and next-step recommendation at week six.

Exclude emergency symptoms, suspected fracture or serious pathology, unstable post-operative cases, cases needing hands-on examination, and any case where the clinician cannot assess safely. The intake must stop the purchase flow and route the person to local care when indicated.

Do not promise “cure,” guaranteed return to sport, diagnosis from video alone, or reimbursement. Show the practitioner’s qualification, jurisdiction, limits of video care, privacy practices, cancellation terms, and the exact package contents before payment.

### Staffing and quality

Pilot with one clinical lead and one backup clinician. Use a standard triage form, inclusion/exclusion checklist, escalation template, documentation minimum, incident log, and peer review of the first 20 cases. Keep clinician access to only the data required for care. The platform can be simple, but the clinical workflow cannot be improvised.

The first version should use existing secure video, scheduling, payment, and documentation components, subject to the legal and data review. Build proprietary software only when a repeated workflow saves clinician time or improves adherence.

## 5. Unit economics

The reproducible model is in [venture_unit_economics.py](../model/venture_unit_economics.py), with generated output in [venture_scenarios.csv](../model/venture_scenarios.csv). Run `python3 docs/venture/model/venture_unit_economics.py` after changing assumptions.

| Scenario | Price | Clinician hours | CAC | First-order contribution | Two-cycle LTV:CAC | Packages per clinician/month | Break-even packages/month at €3k fixed overhead |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Downside | €349 | 4.5 | €140 | **−€212** | **−1.4x** | 24 | Not viable |
| Base | €449 | 4.0 | €70 | €55 | 1.2x | 27 | 36 |
| Upside | €549 | 3.25 | €55 | €247 | 5.5x | 33 | 10 |

The model assumes €70/hour clinician cost, 0.5 support hours per package at €25/hour, 2% payment fees, 5% refunds, 30% repeat purchase at €249, and €3,000 monthly fixed overhead. “LTV” here is a two-repeat contribution proxy, not accounting revenue and not a promise of retention. The base case fails the usual 3x LTV:CAC heuristic, so paid acquisition should remain off until the pilot shows at least €150 first-order contribution or CAC below €45, with no clinical quality regression.

Key sensitivities:

- Every 30 minutes removed from clinician time adds about €35 contribution at €70/hour.
- A €100 price increase adds about €93 after 5% refunds and 2% payment fees.
- A €25 CAC reduction adds €25 directly to first-order contribution.
- Refunds above 10% or clinician time above 4.5 hours should trigger a service redesign.
- A €3,000 fixed-cost stack needs about 36 base-case packages per month, above one clinician’s 27-package capacity. This means either a leaner fixed stack, higher contribution, or a second clinician is needed before growth spending.

### Cash guardrails

Use a €15,000 validation budget over 90 days, released in gates: €3,000 for legal/privacy/insurance setup, €2,000 for the landing page and secure workflow, €5,000 for clinician delivery and research incentives, and up to €5,000 for paid tests only after the first ten paid cases meet safety and satisfaction thresholds. Do not commit to a three-country build, app, or large ad budget inside this amount.

## 6. Distribution strategy

The initial bottleneck is trust and activation, not reach. The customer must believe that remote assessment is safe, the clinician is qualified, and the package will change what they do this week.

1. **High-intent search:** Create German pages for “online Physiotherapie Sportverletzung,” “Läuferknie online,” “Achillessehne Reha online,” “Physio nach Kreuzband OP online,” and “Nackenschmerzen Arbeitsplatz.” Each page needs inclusion limits, clinician credentials, evidence references, price, and a booking CTA. SEO is a compounding channel, but it will take months.
2. **Referral network:** Recruit sports coaches, running clubs, personal trainers, orthopaedic practices, and sports physicians. Give partners a clinical handoff page and tracked referral link. Avoid referral fees where professional rules prohibit them; use education and clear patient choice.
3. **Proof-led content:** Publish short movement explainers, case progression stories with consent, and “when online is not appropriate” content. The latter builds trust and reduces unsafe demand.
4. **Paid search smoke test:** Test only high-intent terms after the checkout and triage flow work. Cap spend at €1,000 per message/segment. Pause a segment after 100 qualified clicks without three booked assessments, or after CAC exceeds €120 without an improving conversion signal.
5. **Sports communities and creators:** Partner with a small number of credible coaches, clubs, and micro-creators. Measure booked assessments and completed packages, not views.
6. **Employer pilots:** Test a desk-worker programme with two employers only after the consumer service is safe and repeatable. This is a possible lower-CAC route, but it changes the buyer and sales cycle.

The loop is: useful public assessment content → self-identification and safe triage → paid assessment → visible progress → consented proof and coach/clinician referral. Make progress review the investment that brings the customer back, rather than adding a content library.

## 7. Riskiest assumption tests and kill criteria

The RAT is: **At least 8 of the first 20 eligible German sports/MSK prospects will pay €399 or more for the six-week package after a clinician-led assessment, complete 70% of planned touchpoints, and produce a positive functional change without a safety incident.**

Run the following before platform build:

| Test | Method | Pass threshold | Kill or pivot rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem reality | 15 Mom Test interviews with athletes who sought physio in the last 12 months. Ask about last injury, alternatives, wait, spend, and what was abandoned. | 10/15 report a recent access or adherence failure and describe a paid workaround. | If fewer than 6, narrow to a different segment. |
| Willingness to pay | Clinician call plus payment link for a €399 founding package, manually delivered. | 8 paid packages from 20 eligible assessed prospects. | If fewer than 4, change segment or promise before building. |
| Remote suitability | Triage 30 leads using the inclusion/exclusion checklist. | ≥80% safely suitable; zero serious incident. | If <70% suitable or any preventable serious incident, restrict indication and seek clinical review. |
| Delivery margin | Track every clinician and support minute for first 20 cases. | Median ≤4.0 clinician hours and ≥€150 first-order contribution at realised CAC. | If median >4.5 hours, redesign the service before more demand. |
| Retention and outcome | Weekly completion, a simple patient-specific functional measure, and week-six interview. | ≥70% touchpoint completion, ≥30% continuation/referral intent, and documented functional improvement in ≥60%. | If completion <50% or improvement <40%, fix adherence/clinical design. |
| Acquisition | Organic, partner, and €1k paid tests separated by source. | Paid CAC ≤€70 by 10 customers or partner CAC ≤€40. | Pause paid media above €120 CAC; double down on the source that produces completed care. |

Mom Test prompts: “Tell me about the last time your training or daily life was limited by pain.” “What did you do in the first week?” “How did you choose the professional or app?” “What did you pay and what did insurance cover?” “What made you stop or continue?” Do not ask whether people like the idea.

## 8. 30/60/90-day plan

**Days 0–30, prove the problem and remove unsafe scope.**

- Founder: conduct 15 interviews and recruit the clinical lead.
- Clinical lead: define inclusion/exclusion, remote tests, documentation, escalation, and outcome measure.
- Legal adviser: confirm German operating model, professional authorization, telehealth, consumer terms, privacy/health-data controls, insurance, tax, and advertising questions.
- Product operator: ship a one-page landing page, manual booking, payment, consent, and a secure delivery workflow.
- Gate: 8 paid founding packages or a documented pivot. No paid acquisition beyond small message tests.

**Days 31–60, run the concierge cohort.**

- Deliver 10–20 packages and log every minute, issue, referral, refund, and outcome.
- Test two positions: sport-specific return to performance and persistent neck/back pain.
- Recruit 10 referral partners and run two small content or creator tests.
- Gate: no preventable serious incident, ≥70% completion, median delivery ≤4 hours, and first-order contribution ≥€100. If not, fix offer or stop.

**Days 61–90, choose the launch wedge.**

- Run a controlled search test capped at €2,000 total, only for the best-performing indication.
- Add one backup clinician and peer review.
- Draft the Austria and Switzerland expansion checklist, but do not sell there until local counsel confirms the cross-border model.
- Gate: 20 paid customers, three consecutive weeks of qualified demand, CAC ≤€70 or a clearly repeatable partner channel, refunds ≤8%, and continuation/referral ≥30%.
- Decision: proceed to a Germany-first launch if all gates pass; pivot segment/price if demand passes but margin fails; stop if willingness to pay or clinical suitability fails.

## 9. Principal risks and controls

| Risk | Early control | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Remote care unsuitable or delayed referral | Red-flag triage, exclusion list, local referral directory, incident review. | Clinical lead |
| Practitioner or entity not authorised in customer’s place | Country-specific counsel before cross-border sale; identify place of care and responsibility. | Founder + counsel |
| Health-data breach | Data-minimised workflow, processor agreements, access controls, retention/deletion schedule, incident plan. | Founder + security counsel |
| Claims breach advertising/medical rules | Evidence-reviewed claims, no guarantees, clear limits and credentials. | Founder + counsel |
| Low willingness to pay | Prepayment test before platform build; price and segment experiments. | Founder |
| Low clinician margin | Minute-level time tracking, package caps, templated education, price floor. | Clinical lead + finance |
| Paid acquisition dependence | Partner and search mix, source-level CAC, hard spend caps. | Growth owner |
| Reputation damage from poor outcome | Selection criteria, quality review, transparent escalation, rapid complaint handling. | Clinical lead |
| Country expansion complexity | Germany-first legal and operational proof; separate Austria/Switzerland launch gates. | Founder |

## Clear recommendation

Proceed with a constrained, Germany-first paid pilot. The opportunity is credible because access is constrained, digital physiotherapy is familiar, and observed private-pay prices support a €449–€549 package. The venture is not yet investable as a broad DACH platform: the base economics are weak, clinical suitability is bounded, and cross-border rules need local advice.

The decisive evidence is not a large market-size estimate. It is 20 paid cases showing safe remote selection, ≤4 clinician hours per package, ≥€150 first-order contribution, ≥70% completion, and a repeat or referral signal. If those numbers are reached, expand the best-performing sports indication in Germany, then test Austria and Switzerland through separately validated practitioner and entity arrangements. If they are not reached after one focused offer and one adjacent segment test, stop or pivot before building software or buying scale.
