# KNIEATHLETICS benchmark brief

**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Recommendation:** deepen Steepstep’s existing routes and proof before adding route count.

## What the benchmark does well

KNIEATHLETICS makes a narrow promise immediately: digital rehabilitation after cruciate ligament and meniscus injuries. The headline is followed by concrete return-to-life examples such as kneeling, squatting, walking, running, stopping and jumping. This gives the visitor a clear outcome before the service explanation begins. [Homepage](https://knieathletics.de/)

The site earns its length through a chain of evidence: specific outcomes; named cases with injury and return activity; a mechanism of individual planning, weekly video movement analysis and personal support; a four-part care journey; a six-level progress model; an FAQ covering cost, insurance, existing treatment, equipment and digital suitability; a named team; and repeated consultation calls to action. See the [homepage](https://knieathletics.de/) and [brochure page](https://knieathletics.de/broschure/).

The brochure page is a useful example of a standalone page with a clear job. It says what the guide contains, includes customer stories, answers questions, and then presents its form. [Broschure](https://knieathletics.de/broschure/)

The benchmark also shows what to avoid: repeated sections, visible spelling errors, placeholder team roles, strong claims without inspectable evidence, and pricing deferred to a consultation. Steepstep should keep the information depth while staying tighter, accurate, and transparent about CHF pricing.

## Steepstep-specific direction

Steepstep should borrow the information architecture, never the benchmark’s claims, wording, imagery or visual identity:

1. specific client outcome;
2. proof that the team has seen the problem before;
3. explanation of the actual method;
4. a tangible journey with deliverables;
5. objections answered in plain language;
6. people and qualifications;
7. one consistent next step.

Keep the existing compact route set. Do not create pages for every sport until there is first-hand expertise, demand and enough content for a real user job. The existing sport routes should become complete editorial service pages rather than card destinations.

### Home

Follow the hero with a concise “for whom” statement, three concrete outcomes, the remote care model, CHF offer summary, and one strong expert proof block. A visitor should know within one screen whether the service fits and within three sections what they receive.

### Offer and how it works

State the actual journey and deliverables: a free 20-minute orientation and fit call, clearly separate from diagnosis; a paid 60-minute clinical assessment at CHF 149; CHF 549 Begleitung and CHF 749 Fortschritt packages with inclusions; the cadence of video feedback and plan adjustments; useful equipment; and when local medical assessment is safer. Pricing and delivery must match across home, offer, FAQ and contact.

### Sport pages

Running should cover load progression, strength and tolerance, and when symptoms need local assessment. Skiing should cover eccentric load, balance, turns, falls, seasonal preparation and return confidence. Each page needs a sport-specific goal, self-contained answer passages, suitability boundaries, a short FAQ, internal links, and distinct licensed imagery. These are content directions, not promises or diagnoses.

### About and trust

Introduce the expert group, then give Natalja Krol a verified profile covering role, physiotherapy and sports focus, relevant experience, and her view of good care. Add the legal operator and Swiss context nearby. Avoid unverified awards, client counts and credentials. Add case stories only with consent and attribution. Until then, use verifiable qualifications and process proof.

### Design

Create depth through alternating human image, short claim, mechanism, proof and action. Make Steepstep’s “next good step” idea ownable with a quiet continuous route line or stepped marker, used as information structure rather than decoration. Use one wide image and one close detail per long page, never repeat a hero, and maintain one content width, header, footer, type scale, CTA language and interaction treatment everywhere. Use restrained entrance reveals, tactile button press states, and pointer-only image shifts. Reduced motion must show the same content without animation.

Do not copy KNIEATHLETICS’ six-level system unless Steepstep clinicians define real criteria. A decorative progress model would imply clinical authority without substance.
