# Switzerland online physiotherapy market refresh

**Research date:** 12 September 2026
**Scope:** Swiss private-pay and privately insured customers; online physiotherapy and adjacent movement-health offers.
**Use:** Evidence input for the venture decision and later product and website work. This memo does not replace canton-specific legal advice.

## Executive finding

Switzerland is a credible first market for a specialist, private-pay online physiotherapy service. The need is large: 45% of the population reported back or kidney pain in 2022, 36% reported shoulder, neck or arm pain, and musculoskeletal disorders were the second most common hospitalisation category after injuries. The opportunity is strongest where the patient values a fast, expert decision and a structured return to activity, rather than a generic exercise library.

The belief that Switzerland supports higher prices is **verified in the local private-care benchmark, but limited for online care**. Published Swiss private clinic prices cluster around CHF 130–148 for 50–60 minutes. Published online prices found in this scan range from CHF 59 for a 30-minute follow-up to CHF 89 for a 45-minute first session. A Swiss online provider can therefore test premium packages, but should not assume that online sessions inherit full in-clinic pricing.

Recommended market entry: Switzerland first, one language region first, with a sports and active-lifestyle wedge. Sell a defined outcome package with clinician continuity, baseline assessment, personalised progression, video feedback, and clear safety escalation. Keep persistent back and neck pain as a second testable segment, because demand is broad but competition and substitution are also broad.

## Demand signals

### Musculoskeletal need

- The Federal Statistical Office reports back or kidney pain among 45% of the Swiss population in 2022. The same source reports 40.1% of men and 50.0% of women affected, and 36% with shoulder, neck or arm pain. Source: [FSO, Population health 2022](https://www.swissstats.bfs.admin.ch/data/webviewer/appId/ch.admin.bfs.swissstat/article/issue241415402400-03/package), accessed 12 September 2026.
- The FSO reports 70,000 men and 83,396 women hospitalised for musculoskeletal disorders in 2022. Joint disease accounted for 53% and back disorders for 23% of musculoskeletal hospitalisation reasons. This is a need signal, not a direct estimate of addressable online demand. Source: [FSO, Population health 2022](https://www.swissstats.bfs.admin.ch/data/webviewer/appId/ch.admin.bfs.swissstat/article/issue241415402400-03/package), accessed 12 September 2026.
- The Swiss health survey covered about 22,000 people in 2022, giving a useful national population signal rather than a clinic sample. Source: [Obsan indicator, back and headache pain](https://ind.obsan.admin.ch/indicator/obsan/_050), accessed 12 September 2026.

### Sport and injury need

- Suva recorded about 296,000 recognised leisure accidents in 2025, up 2.8%. It identifies football, skiing, and mountain hiking as the most frequent sports accident contexts, with knee, ankle, and shoulder injuries among the common injury locations. Source: [Suva, 2025 accident figures](https://www.suva.ch/de-ch/ueber-uns/magazin-und-medien/medien/suva-unfallzahlen-2025), accessed 12 September 2026.
- The sports signal supports a focused offer for runners, skiers, footballers, hikers, and cyclists. It does not prove that each group will pay online. The first validation should measure paid conversion by sport and the proportion requiring in-person assessment.
- BASPO’s Sport Schweiz programme shows sport participation and interest are actively monitored nationally, with regional differences across German-, French-, and Italian-speaking Switzerland. Source: [BASPO, Sport Schweiz](https://www.baspo.admin.ch/de/sport-schweiz), accessed 12 September 2026.

### Demand interpretation

The best initial demand hypothesis is not “online physiotherapy for everyone.” It is “a qualified Swiss physiotherapist helps an active person make a confident recovery decision and follow through at home.” The job includes triage, diagnosis-informed exercise selection, progression, and return-to-sport confidence. The online format removes travel and makes adherence visible, but it cannot supply hands-on examination or treatment.

## Market gaps and segment hypotheses

| Segment | Unmet job | Why online may fit | Main risk | Test offer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active adults with running, cycling, skiing, football or hiking pain | Stay active or return safely without losing weeks to waiting and travel | Movement can be observed at home; programmes can be sport-specific | Red flags, post-operative cases, or instability need physical assessment | 6-week Return to Sport package, CHF 449–649 hypothesis |
| Desk-based professionals with recurring back, neck, and shoulder pain | Reduce recurrence and keep working without repeated generic appointments | Home workstation and movement context are directly observable | High competition from clinics, apps, exercise videos, and employer services | 4-week Workday Mobility reset, CHF 249–399 hypothesis |
| Post-discharge or post-clinic continuity | Continue a known plan with accountability after in-person care | Digital follow-up and adherence are a natural fit | Referral and handoff responsibility must be explicit | Clinician-to-clinician continuity package, test only with a referral partner |
| International professionals in Switzerland | Receive care in English or another familiar language | National reach without local travel | Licensing, scope, and emergency location still matter | English-language sports assessment, premium price test |
| Women returning to activity after pregnancy or pelvic-health rehabilitation | Progress safely with privacy and continuity | Home sessions and asynchronous check-ins reduce travel friction | Specialist competence and referral boundaries are essential | Only after specialist capability and referral network are proven |

The sports wedge is the clearest fit with the team’s stated strength. The back and neck segment is the highest-volume adjacent test. Pelvic health and post-operative care may be attractive, but require explicit specialist capability and tighter clinical pathways.

## Competitor and price scan

### Published Swiss price anchors

- Physioline lists private physiotherapy at CHF 80 for up to 30 minutes and CHF 148 for up to 60 minutes, plus travel charges for home visits. Source: [Physioline prices](https://www.physioline.ch/preise/), accessed 12 September 2026.
- PhysioDietikon lists CHF 70 for 25 minutes and CHF 140 for 50 minutes when privately billed. Source: [PhysioDietikon prices](https://www.physiodietikon.ch/preise), accessed 12 September 2026.
- Kuoni Training und Therapie lists CHF 65 for 30 minutes, CHF 97.50 for 45 minutes, and CHF 130 for 60 minutes for privately paid physiotherapy. Source: [Kuoni prices](https://www.kuoni-zentrum.ch/infos/preise), accessed 12 September 2026.
- physio HOLISTIC lists CHF 70 for 30 minutes and CHF 140 for 60 minutes for private patients. Source: [physio HOLISTIC prices](https://www.physio-holistic.ch/preise), accessed 12 September 2026.
- Physioproalp lists online prices from CHF 50 / EUR 55 for 30 minutes, with video or audio consultation and exercise instruction. Source: [Physioproalp online physiotherapy](https://www.physioproalp.ch/physio-online), accessed 12 September 2026.
- Digiphysio in Zurich lists a free 15-minute fit call, CHF 89 for a 45-minute first online appointment, and CHF 59 for a 30-minute online follow-up. It also lists a CHF 299 120-minute running assessment in person. Source: [Digiphysio booking page](https://tbooking.ch/de/frank-arends/4896-6049), accessed 12 September 2026.

### Positioning map

The visible market has four groups:

1. Traditional practices that sell reimbursable or private time, often with online booking but limited online-first positioning.
2. Individual tele-physio practitioners who sell convenience and low-friction video consultations, often at CHF 50–90.
3. Large clinics and sports-medicine brands that own strong trust and referral channels, with digital exercise follow-up as an adjunct.
4. Apps and digital therapy products that offer low-cost guidance but less human clinical judgement.

The open position is a clinician-led, Swiss-trust, outcome-based service between groups 2 and 3: more specific and accountable than a video call, more accessible and personal than a hospital or clinic system, and more clinically credible than an exercise app.

### Pricing recommendation

Test three packages with payment collected before care begins:

- **Assessment, CHF 149–189:** 60-minute video assessment, written plan, red-flag screening, and next-step recommendation.
- **Guided recovery, CHF 449–649:** six weeks, one assessment, four 30-minute follow-ups, weekly plan updates, asynchronous video feedback, and a clear outcome measure.
- **Performance return, CHF 749–990:** eight to ten weeks, sport-specific testing, more frequent feedback, and a clinician-defined return-to-sport protocol. Use only for cases that fit online care.

These are hypotheses, not market facts. They intentionally sit above the CHF 59–89 online session anchor and around or below the cost of several comparable in-person sessions. The test should use a Van Westendorp-style price interview after the customer has described a recent paid care episode, then a real checkout test. Do not infer willingness to pay from stated enthusiasm.

## Swiss legal and operating context

The team has stated that its practitioners are trained, professionally qualified, and entitled to work in the profession. The relevant operating task is to document the exact Swiss entity, practitioner, canton, service location, and patient-location model before launch.

- Physiotherapy is a profession covered by the Swiss Health Professions Act, GesBG. A person working under their own professional responsibility needs a professional practice authorisation from the canton in which the profession is exercised. The Federal Office of Public Health states that cantons issue the authorisation and supervise practice. Source: [BAG GesBG FAQ](https://www.bag.admin.ch/bag/de/home/berufe-im-gesundheitswesen/gesundheitsberufe-der-tertiaerstufe/faq-bundesgesetz-ueber-die-gesundheitsberufe.html), accessed 12 September 2026.
- The federal framework does not remove cantonal practical differences. The BAG explicitly recommends checking with the relevant cantonal authorities. For an online service, confirm which canton is treated as the place of practice, whether the patient’s canton creates an additional requirement, and what rules apply when a patient is temporarily abroad. Source: [BAG, health professions legislation](https://www.bag.admin.ch/bag/de/home/gesetze-und-bewilligungen/gesetzgebung/gesetzgebung-berufe-im-gesundheitswesen/gesetzgebung-gesundheitsberufe.html), accessed 12 September 2026.
- Swiss health data are specially protected personal data under the Federal Data Protection Act. The service needs a documented purpose, minimum necessary collection, access controls, retention and deletion rules, processor agreements, breach handling, and a patient-facing privacy notice. Source: [Fedlex, Data Protection Act](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en), accessed 12 September 2026.
- If the business wants to bill compulsory health insurance, the requirements for recognised providers and prescribed treatment become central. A private-pay proposition can be simpler, but the website must state plainly what is and is not reimbursed and avoid implying basic-insurance coverage. Source: [BAG, providers and authorisation](https://www.bag.admin.ch/bag/de/home/versicherungen/krankenversicherung/leistungserbringer.html), accessed 12 September 2026.
- Swiss tariff structures for prescribed ambulatory physiotherapy are separate from a private cash price. The BAG publishes the national physiotherapy tariff structure; its existence is not evidence that an online package will be reimbursed. Source: [BAG, physiotherapy tariff structure](https://www.bag.admin.ch/bag/de/home/versicherungen/krankenversicherung/krankenversicherung-leistungen-tarife/Nicht-aerztliche-Leistungen/Physiotherapie.html), accessed 12 September 2026.

Operationally, the website and intake must include a clinical suitability screen, emergency and red-flag instructions, patient location at each session, consent for video and asynchronous messaging, identity and contact details, cancellation terms, and a referral path for physical examination. The service should use Swiss-hosted or contractually reviewed infrastructure where practical, and should avoid recording sessions by default.

## Distribution opportunities

The best first channels are trust-rich and intent-led:

- Partnerships with running clubs, ski schools, football clubs, climbing gyms, cycling communities, and premium fitness studios. Offer a screening workshop or injury-prevention session that leads into a paid assessment.
- Search content for specific high-intent problems such as “Knieschmerzen beim Laufen,” “wieder joggen nach Sprunggelenksverletzung,” and “online Physio Schweiz.” Each page should explain suitability and limits, then route to a fit call or assessment.
- Referral relationships with sports physicians, orthopaedic clinics, personal trainers, and coaches for continuity after the patient no longer needs hands-on care.
- Employer partnerships for desk-based pain only after the consumer offer works. Employers can reduce acquisition cost but add procurement time and reporting constraints.
- Patient proof based on functional outcomes, such as return to a run, hike, match, or workday. Avoid unsupported claims such as “pain-free in six weeks.”

Paid social should be used for creative and audience learning, not as the sole growth engine. A premium healthcare service needs repeated evidence of practitioner quality, transparent credentials, a clear safety boundary, and visible Swiss contact details.

## Riskiest assumptions and validation plan

The riskiest assumption is: **Swiss active adults will pay at least CHF 449 for a structured online recovery package from a provider without a famous clinic brand, and enough cases can be managed safely online.**

Run a four-week concierge test before building a platform:

1. Interview 10 people who paid privately for physiotherapy, sports rehabilitation, coaching, or medical assessment in the last 12 months. Ask what triggered the purchase, what they paid, what was missing, and what happened after treatment.
2. Recruit 20 qualified Swiss leads through two sports communities and one search landing page. Offer a paid CHF 149 assessment, then present the package at CHF 549.
3. Deliver 8–12 assessments and at least 6 packages manually. Track suitability, conversion, attendance, clinician time, functional outcome, referral need, and refund requests.
4. Pass criteria: at least 8 paid assessments, at least 4 package purchases, at least 70% package completion, positive change on a predeclared functional measure, no serious safety event, and clinician time under 5 hours per package.

Pivot or pause if leads will only buy single sessions below CHF 100, if more than 25% require in-person assessment, if completion is below 60%, or if acquisition depends on discounts that erase contribution margin.

## What is verified, what remains unknown

Verified enough for strategy: Switzerland has substantial musculoskeletal and sports-injury need; private in-person physiotherapy prices are materially higher than the visible online session prices; Swiss professional practice and health-data obligations are real and canton-sensitive; online competition exists but remains fragmented.

Limited: a national online physiotherapy market size, true willingness to pay for packages, conversion rates by language region, insurer reimbursement for the exact service, and the scale of demand among international professionals. These require primary customer tests and written advice for the exact operating setup.
